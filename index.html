getting started
welcome to bsp

The Bitcraft Settlement Planner lets you design settlement layouts on a hex grid before building them in-game. Paint terrain, place buildings with accurate footprints, terraform elevation, and preview everything in 3D.

The left panel has four collapsible cards. Press 1-4 to jump to a panel, or click any header. The active card determines your mode - opening buildings enters stamp mode, opening terrain enters terraform mode, and everything else is paint mode.

The status bar at the bottom shows your current mode, cursor coordinates, and the 3d view toggle.

moving around

The viewport is your workspace. Navigate with the mouse:

middle-drag
pan the view
scroll
zoom in/out
i
invert scroll direction

The grid starts at 100x100 hexes. You can resize it in the grid panel (4) if you need more or less space. Coordinates are shown in the status bar as axial Q, R - the same system the game uses internally.

If you get lost, zoom out with scroll to find your bearings.
painting terrain

This is the default mode. Pick a color from the palette (1) and click or drag on the grid to paint hexes. Mark out residential areas, farms, roads, districts - whatever your layout needs.

click
paint a hex
drag
paint an area
+ / -
brush size
dbl-click
add a text label
ctrl+z
undo last action

The palette card also has boundaries and heightmap toggles. These are independent overlays that work in any mode.

The eraser (checkerboard swatch) resets hexes to default. The border swatch renders as a dashed outline for marking claim edges.
placing buildings

Open the buildings card (x) to enter stamp mode. The building picker appears with a category dropdown covering all ~600 buildings from the game.

Select a building, then click on the grid to place it. The hover preview shows the exact hitbox shape from game data for accurate collision planning.

x
open buildings (stamp mode)
q
rotate CCW (60 deg)
e / r
rotate CW (60 deg)
Switching to another panel automatically exits stamp mode. No need to toggle anything off first.
selecting & moving buildings

Once buildings are placed, you can select, move, rotate, and delete them:

shift+click
select a building (highlights yellow)
shift+click
on empty space - reposition selected building
q / e
rotate selected building in-place
del
delete selected building
esc
deselect and return to palette
All building operations support undo with ctrl+z.
terraforming

Open the terrain card (t) to enter terraform mode. Boundary lines appear automatically so you can see the 7-hex tile clusters. Click clusters to select them, then use the depth slider (0-100).

t
open terrain (terraform mode)
click
select a tile cluster
ctrl+click
multi-select tiles
esc
clear selection and return to palette

The baseline depth button sets a default for all tiles at once. Spacer tiles between clusters inherit depth from neighbors via majority-vote - just like in-game.

Terraforming is optional. If you just want a flat layout with buildings, skip it.
3d visualization

Press v or click 3d in the status bar to switch to perspective rendering. Terrain extrudes based on depth values, and buildings render as colored prisms.

v
toggle 3D view
left-drag
orbit camera
scroll
zoom
h
toggle heightmap colors
b
toggle boundary lines
Terraform editing requires 2D view. Painting and building placement work in both.
saving & loading

Plans save as JSON files to your computer. Nothing is stored on a server - your plans are entirely local.

save
downloads current plan as .json
load
opens a file picker to load a saved plan
reset
clears everything (with confirmation)

The plan name in the top bar sets the filename. Plans include all painted hexes, buildings with rotation, depth values, and labels. Share .json files with other players - they just load them into their own BSP.

ctrl+z undoes the last 100 actions. Save and reset clear the undo stack.
